## Summary
I found no current, in-repo naming or design guide for the Rocq standard library. `rocq-prover/stdlib`'s `CONTRIBUTING.md` is about process (issues, PRs, CI, changelog) and a grep of the repo for "naming convention" found nothing relevant. `dev/doc/style.md` in `rocq-prover/rocq` exists but is about the OCaml sources of the prover (title only seen; **not read**). The only public style doc found is the old CoqStyle wiki page, which is short and mostly about file and proof layout. It defers lemma naming to "the pattern used for similar lemmas in the Coq standard library", i.e. the convention is learned by imitation.

## Rules
> For lemmas try to follow a uniform naming convention based on the subject of the formalisation. If in doubt use the naming pattern used for similar lemmas that is used in the Coq standard library.
([CoqStyle, Naming](https://github.com/coq/coq/wiki/CoqStyle))

> It is good practice to use a uniform naming convention for inductive data-types, their constructors, the inverse of the constructors (provable by \`inversion\`) and the strong elimination rules (generated by \`Scheme\`).
(same)

> It is usually a good idea to explicitly give names to all the hypothesis that are introduced by Coq. This makes reuse of the code easier.
(same, "Variables inside proofs")

> Use [coqdoc] style comments graciously, to clarify the structure of your formalisation. (However, try not to use them *inside* proofs.)
(same, "File Structure")

> Start the files with a comment [...] This will credit the author(s) and clarifies any license issues, which is necessary for re-usability of your code.
(same)

## Reasons given
Hypothesis naming: easier reuse. Licence header: reusability. Otherwise none.

## What transfers to general-purpose Lean
- "Name hypotheses explicitly instead of relying on generated names" is the same robustness point Lean's tactic-proof section makes about inaccessible names; it also appears in QED at Large (see \`proof-engineering.md\`).
- "Imitate the stdlib" is the implicit policy for naming in a mature library; a Lean guide has the advantage of a written naming doc.
- The page is about Coq 8.x-era scripts; most layout rules do not transfer.
